# Project Overview

This repository contains my Digital Literacy Portfolio Project completed as part of the CSE0001 Digital Literacy course. The objective of this project is to develop practical digital skills that are essential for students in the modern academic and professional environment. Digital literacy includes the ability to effectively use online tools, maintain a professional digital presence, communicate responsibly, and understand cybersecurity risks.

The project consists of five tasks that focus on different aspects of digital literacy. These tasks involve creating awareness content, building professional profiles, exploring coding and collaboration platforms, practicing professional email communication, and researching cybercrime prevention strategies. All the work has been documented and organized in this repository to demonstrate responsible and effective use of digital technologies.

This repository also serves as a starting point for my long-term professional digital portfolio as a student of Artificial Intelligence and Bioinformatics.

# Task 1 – Digital Literacy Awareness Infographic

In this task, an infographic was created to spread awareness about digital literacy among students. The infographic highlights key topics such as the meaning of digital literacy, safe internet practices, useful digital tools for students, and the importance of maintaining a professional online presence.

A visual design tool like Canva was used to create the infographic. The final design was exported and added to the task-1-presentation folder. This task helped in understanding how visual communication can be used to simplify complex information and spread awareness effectively.

# Task 2 – Student Digital Portfolio

This task involved creating a professional digital presence across important online platforms used in academic and technical communities. Profiles were created or updated on platforms such as GitHub, LinkedIn, and Kaggle.

A profile README was also created on GitHub to introduce myself, my academic program, and my learning interests. Screenshots of the profiles were collected and added to the task-2-portfolio folder. This task emphasizes the importance of maintaining a professional online identity that can support future internships, collaborations, and career opportunities.

# Task 3 – Coding and Collaboration Platforms

This task introduced tools that support coding practice and academic collaboration.

For coding practice, a beginner-level challenge was completed on HackerRank, which helped in understanding how coding platforms help students improve their problem-solving skills.

For collaboration, a quiz was created using Google Forms to test digital literacy awareness among students. The responses were linked to Google Sheets to demonstrate how online forms can be used for surveys, quizzes, and data collection in educational environments.

Screenshots and activity proof are stored in the task-3-platforms folder.

# Task 4 – Email Etiquette and Social Media Responsibility

This task focuses on professional digital communication. Two professional email drafts were written:

A formal email requesting an assignment submission extension from a professor.
An email expressing interest in a summer internship opportunity.

Additionally, a Social Media Do's and Don'ts checklist was created to highlight responsible online behavior for college students. These materials are stored in the task-4-email-etiquette folder.

This task demonstrates how proper communication and online conduct are essential for maintaining professionalism in academic and professional environments.

# Task 5 – Cybercrime Awareness Case Study

The final task focuses on cybersecurity awareness. A case study on Phishing, one of the most common cybercrimes, was researched and documented. The case study explains how phishing attacks work, who they target, and what consequences they can have for individuals and organizations.

A prevention checklist was also created to provide practical tips for staying safe online, especially for students using digital platforms and online payment systems.

Students are encouraged to report cybercrime incidents through the National Cyber Crime Portal or by calling the 1930 cybercrime helpline.

All materials related to this task are available in the task-5-cybercrime folder.
